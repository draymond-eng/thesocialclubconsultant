The story

I left finance to build Three Cities Social.

What most adults are missing isn't ambition. It's connection.

On leaving Merrill Lynch

So I built Three Cities Social in River North, designed around one idea: make it easy to see the same people again. It worked. The club grew from 25 founding members to 250+. It runs 30-45 events a month.

The timeline, honestly

  1. July 2024

    Opened in River North with 25 founding members.

  2. May 2025

    250+ members, still effectively a one-person operation.

  3. Today

    250+ members and 30-45 events a month. Going deeper, not just bigger: capped membership and sharper programming.

Knowing when to close is part of the job.

I opened a second location, and I closed it on purpose. When the numbers said walk, I walked away from a ~$1.4M lease renewal, and the club is stronger for it. Most consultants only sell the growth story. I'll help you know when to expand, and when to stop.

What I believe

The Ten Laws

Ten rules the club actually runs on, each one paid for before it was written down. Here is the first. The other nine belong to clients and the Circle.

Law I

Charge to show up.

Free things get treated like free things. Attach even a small, refundable commitment and attendance transforms.

Receipt: Our free kickball team of 25 forfeited about 80% of its games. Our deposit-backed volleyball league never forfeited once. Same members.

Laws II through X

Pricing, programming, culture, growth, and the one about money. Each with the receipt that taught it. Clients and the Circle only.
